Chapter VIII

The American Revolution

Causes, course, and consequences of the war, 1775–1783.

By the spring of 1776, the political break between the thirteen American colonies and Great Britain had become irreconcilable.

The dispute, which had begun over the right to tax, had expanded within a decade to questions of sovereignty, representation, and the meaning of subjecthood itself. The First Continental Congress, meeting in Philadelphia in September 1774, had attempted a petition to the Crown. The petition went unanswered.

The Second Continental Congress, which would become both a governing body and a wartime assembly, was already meeting when, on the night of April 18, militia riders set out from Boston to warn the countryside that British regulars were marching to seize a cache of arms at Concord.

The war that followed would last eight years, span a continent, and end in a victory so improbable that the most serious contemporary observers had given the colonial cause no chance of surviving the first campaign.

Date 1775–1783
Colonies Thirteen, of which all but one sent delegates.
Also Continental Congress, Lexington, Yorktown, Treaty of Paris.
